## Bafana Bafana Coaching Carousel: Pitso Mosimane's Return Ignites Hope and Debate

Hold the phone, South African football fans! A familiar, yet polarizing, figure is reportedly set to return to the Bafana Bafana hot seat. **Pitso Mosimane**, the man who has etched his name in African club football history, is being tapped by the South African Football Association (SAFA) for a second stint as head coach. This isn't just another coaching change; it's a narrative steeped in redemption, past blunders, and monumental triumphs. SAFA President Danny Jordaan has confirmed Mosimane as their pick, with a formal announcement expected next week, replacing the recently departed Hugo Broos.

Why does this buzz matter so much? Because Mosimane's last tenure with the national team ended in a spectacular, almost unbelievable, mishap. But since then, "Jingles" has transformed into one of the continent's most decorated managers.

### Revisiting Pitso Mosimane's First Stint: The Infamous AFCON Qualification Blunder

Mosimane first took the reins of Bafana Bafana after the 2010 World Cup, stepping out of the shadow of Brazilian legend Carlos Alberto Parreira, whom he'd understudied. However, his initial spell came to an abrupt end in mid-2012, marred by a disappointing start to the 2014 World Cup qualifiers and, more famously, a calamitous exit from the 2012 Africa Cup of Nations qualification campaign.

In a moment etched in infamy, Mosimane and his players celebrated a 0-0 draw against Sierra Leone, believing it was enough to qualify for AFCON. They had completely misunderstood the tie-breaker rules, which prioritized head-to-head records over goal difference. South Africa actually needed a win. The celebratory dancing quickly turned to collective despair and national embarrassment. It was a tactical miscalculation of epic proportions, ultimately costing him his job.

### From Blunder to Brilliance: Mosimane's Redemption Arc in Club Football

After that very public stumble, many wondered if Mosimane's career would ever recover. But what followed was a phenomenal redemption story. He returned to club football, taking over at **Mamelodi Sundowns**, and transformed them into continental giants. In 2016, he led them to the CAF Champions League title, silencing his critics and proving his tactical genius.

His success didn't stop there. In 2020, Egyptian powerhouse **Al Ahly** poached him, and under his guidance, they reached three consecutive Champions League finals, lifting the trophy in 2020 and 2021. He cemented his status as Africa's most decorated club coach, a serial winner who knows how to navigate the complex world of continental competitions. Mosimane also enjoyed spells managing clubs in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iran, broadening his international experience.

### What's Next for Bafana Bafana Under Pitso Mosimane? A New Era Beckons

Now, at 62, Mosimane is poised to return to the national fold, replacing Belgian-born Hugo Broos, 74, who recently stepped down after South Africa's last-32 exit at this year's World Cup to co-hosts Canada.

Mosimane's immediate task will be to kick off the next **Africa Cup of Nations qualifying campaign**. His first matches are set to be home against Guinea and away against Eritrea next month, crucial encounters on the road to next year's tournament in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda.

The pressure is immense, the expectations sky-high. Can Mosimane translate his unparalleled club success to the national stage? Can he overcome the ghosts of 2012 and guide Bafana Bafana to consistent qualification and deep runs in major tournaments?

## The Buzz: Can Pitso Mosimane Deliver the Glory South Africa Demands?

This re-appointment represents a massive gamble and an even bigger opportunity for both Mosimane and South African football. It's a chance for a coach who has learned from his past and conquered Africa at club level to finally deliver the consistent success that Bafana Bafana fans have yearned for. The narrative of redemption is compelling, but ultimately, only results will tell if this second chapter will be a triumph or another tale of what-ifs. The footballing world is watching.
