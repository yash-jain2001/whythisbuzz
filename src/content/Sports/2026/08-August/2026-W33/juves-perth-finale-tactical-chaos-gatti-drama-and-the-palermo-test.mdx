The curtain is coming down on Juventus’ ambitious global summer tour, and they are finishing it with a flourish in Western Australia. After making stops in Hong Kong to sink Chelsea and battling Inter in an all-Italian showcase down under, the *Bianconeri* are facing off against Filippo Inzaghi's Palermo at Perth Stadium. 

But while the fans in Perth are bringing the noise, the real drama is unfolding in the team sheets and the medical tent. 

Here is why this final pre-season friendly is a must-watch—and what it tells us about the season ahead.

---

### The Gatti Dilemma: Precaution or Transfer Signal?

The biggest news before kickoff centered around defender **Federico Gatti**. Initially named in the starting lineup, Gatti was forced to pull out at the last minute during warm-ups due to sudden muscle fatigue. 

**Why this matters:** 
While the official line is "precaution," Gatti is currently one of the hottest names in the Serie A transfer market. In modern football, a sudden pre-match omission for a player heavily linked with a move away always raises eyebrows. Whether it is a genuine physical hiccup or a strategic move to protect a valuable transfer asset, Gatti's absence will only accelerate the transfer rumor mill back in Italy.

---

### Spalletti’s Mad Lab: A Mind-Bending Lineup

If you looked closely at Luciano Spalletti’s starting XI for this match, you might have done a double-take. 

* **Juventus XI (4-2-3-1):** Perin; Celik, Gatti (replaced), Koopmeiners, Cabal; Douglas Luiz, McKennie; Conceicao, Alajbegovic, Yildiz; Kolo Muani.

Spalletti is treating this final friendly like a tactical laboratory. **Teun Koopmeiners** starting in what appears to be a deeper defensive role, **Zeki Celik** on the flank, and **Randal Kolo Muani** spearheading a fluid attack supported by the creative spark of Kenan Yildiz. This is a high-octane, experimental blueprint that suggests Juventus wants to play aggressive, possession-heavy, versatile football this season.

---

### Palermo: The Perfect Ambition Check

Palermo isn't just here to be a sparring partner. Under the guidance of Pippo Inzaghi, the Sicilian side is widely tipped as one of the most ambitious teams in Serie B, desperate to secure promotion back to the top flight.

* **Palermo XI (4-2-3-1):** Joronen; Cassandro, Ceccaroni, Magnani, Augello; Segre, Ranocchia; Gyasi, Hernani, Johnsen; Pohjanpalo.

With Joel Pohjanpalo leading the line and a robust midfield featuring Filippo Ranocchia, Palermo represents a highly physical, organized, and motivated opponent. For Juventus, this is the ultimate test of their tactical progression before they fly back to Turin.
