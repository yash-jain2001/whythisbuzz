From fourteen wins to sixty in two seasons? That's not just a rebuild; it's a basketball miracle. And at the heart of the Detroit Pistons' astonishing resurgence is President of Basketball Operations, Trajan Langdon. Now, with expectations soaring for the 2026-27 campaign, Langdon isn't resting on his laurels. He's got a clear mission: inject elite shooting and reliable ball-handling into a playoff-tested roster.

### Trajan Langdon's Strategic Offseason Vision for the Pistons

Langdon's genius lies in his ability to sculpt a roster without sacrificing long-term flexibility. He's made it crystal clear: the Pistons will explore all avenues – trades, free agency – but won't be mortgaging their future for a quick fix. The goal is to address the team's postseason shortcomings, primarily a need for more consistent shooting and playmaking, while maintaining financial agility. This strategic patience opens up a fascinating pool of talent. So, who's on the radar as Detroit prepares for a pivotal offseason?

Here are five potential targets that could redefine the Pistons' championship aspirations:

### Tyler Herro: The Dynamic Scoring & Creation Hub Detroit Needs

Whispers from Langdon's pre-draft availability hinted at the Pistons acting as a third team in a potential blockbuster. With Giannis Antetokounmpo's rumored departure from the Milwaukee Bucks, the league is buzzing, and many believe Detroit could facilitate a major trade involving the Miami Heat. This scenario brings Tyler Herro squarely into focus.

Herro is almost a perfect fit for what the Pistons are seeking. He's an **All-Star caliber scorer** capable of creating his own shot and spacing the floor, making him an ideal co-star for Cade Cunningham. Since entering the league, Herro has been a consistent **3-point threat**, boasting a 38.2% career average from deep while putting up 19.5 points across 394 games. Beyond his scoring, he's developed into a legitimate secondary creator, offering the ball-handling and playmaking Detroit craves. With just one year and $33 million left on his contract, acquiring Herro wouldn't cripple the Pistons' future financial flexibility.

### Trey Murphy III: Elite 3-and-D Wing to Boost the Pistons' Defense

For many fans, Trey Murphy III is at the top of the offseason wish list, and for good reason. He embodies the modern **"3-and-D" archetype** at an elite level, a skill set invaluable in today's NBA.

This past season saw Murphy average a career-best 21.5 points, shooting an impressive 37.9% from beyond the arc. His offensive game is seamlessly integrated into any system, as he doesn't demand the ball to be effective, evidenced by his remarkable 42.6% on catch-and-shoot 3-pointers. Standing at 6-foot-8, Murphy's defensive versatility allows him to guard multiple positions, bringing the kind of energy and hustle that perfectly aligns with the Pistons' defensive identity. He proved his defensive chops with 1.5 steals per game and 24 total blocks last season. The catch? Murphy, with three years left on his deal, will likely be the most expensive target, as the Pelicans still view him as a core piece.

### Zach LaVine: Can He Be the Pistons' Dynamic Secondary Scorer?

The links between Zach LaVine and the Pistons go back years, sparked notably by his 51-point eruption at Little Caesars Arena in October 2023. It’s easy to see why the connection persists.

LaVine brings the kind of **reliable secondary scoring** that could perfectly complement Cade Cunningham, and on any given night, he has the talent to carry the offense himself. The two-time All-Star remains one of the league's most efficient **three-level scorers**. Even in what was considered a "down year" with the 60-loss Sacramento Kings, LaVine averaged 19.2 points, shooting a highly efficient 47.9% from the field and 39% from deep.

Crucially for the Pistons, the Kings appear ready to hit the reset button, making LaVine's expiring $49 million salary for the upcoming season a potential asset they'd be eager to offload. This financial situation makes LaVine one of the **most affordable impact players** to acquire via trade, and his expiring deal would preserve Detroit's future cap space – a win-win scenario for Langdon's strategic vision.

### Klay Thompson: Veteran 3-Point Snipers to Bolster Detroit's Bench

With the Dallas Mavericks seemingly committed to building around Rookie of the Year Cooper Flagg, veteran Klay Thompson could become available for a relatively modest package of second-round picks and a young prospect.

At 36, Thompson might not be the perennial All-Star he once was with the Golden State Warriors, but his legendary shooting touch remains. He played 69 games this past season, averaging 11.7 points and knocking down 202 3-pointers at a respectable 38.3% clip. His addition would immediately **boost the Pistons' bench production** and, more importantly, drastically improve their **floor spacing**, a top priority this offseason. Thompson still has plenty of splash left in the tank, evidenced by his 25 games last season with four or more threes, including a seven-deep-ball performance in March. His expiring $17.4 million contract further enhances Detroit's financial flexibility, making him a low-risk, high-reward option.

### Anthony Davis: The Two-Way Star Who Could Define the Pistons' Future

Though an "honorable mention" due to his recent trade to the Washington Wizards, Anthony Davis was heavily linked to the Pistons previously. While public sentiment often focused on his injury history, it's undeniable that a healthy Davis would be a **postseason difference-maker**.

Davis remains one of the NBA's most dominant **two-way players**, a prolific paint protector who can also score efficiently both inside and out. Despite recent reassurances from Wizards president Michael Winger about Davis's commitment to Washington, the uncertainty surrounding his tenure means a trade before next season isn't entirely off the table. Acquiring a player of Davis's caliber, with two years left on his contract, would be a major move with significant implications, though the cost would undoubtedly be substantial.

### The Buzz: What These Moves Mean for the Pistons' Future

Langdon's meticulous strategy isn't just about adding talent; it's about optimizing an already formidable squad. Whether through a high-impact scorer like Herro, a versatile wing like Murphy, a dynamic veteran like LaVine or Thompson, or even a superstar like Davis, the Pistons are poised to make moves that could truly shake up the league. The message is clear: Detroit is no longer just building; they're *contending*.
