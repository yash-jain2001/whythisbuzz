The 2026 NFL season is on the horizon, and as the Las Vegas Raiders gear up for training camp, all eyes are turning to the trenches – and more specifically, to the explosive potential brewing in their running back room. This isn't just about accumulating talent; it's about crafting a dynamic, multi-faceted ground attack perfectly tailored for Coach Klint Kubiak's wide zone system. The Silver and Black aren't just looking for a lead back; they're assembling a stable designed to wear down defenses and ignite their offense.

## Mike Washington Jr.: The Explosive Rookie Ready to Burst Onto the Scene

Perhaps the most intriguing new addition to the Raiders' backfield is **Mike Washington Jr.**, the fourth-round pick from Arkansas. Trading up on Day 3 to snag him speaks volumes about the organization's belief in his potential. And when you look at his profile, it's easy to see why.

Washington is a rare blend of size and electrifying speed. At 6-foot-2 and 228 pounds, he still managed to clock the fastest 40-yard dash (4.33 seconds) and 10-yard split (1.51 seconds) among all running backs at this year's Combine. These aren't just impressive numbers; they scream "home-run threat." While he only spent one season in a Power Four conference, his collegiate resume — nearly 3,000 rushing yards and 29 scrimmage touchdowns across Buffalo, New Mexico State, and Arkansas — demonstrates consistent, high-level productivity.

Raiders director of college scouting Brandon Yeargan lauded Washington as "super productive, high character," emphasizing his perfect fit for Kubiak and Andrew Janocko's wide zone scheme. This isn't just about raw talent; it's about finding the *right* talent to execute a specific vision. Washington's ability to hit holes quickly and explode downfield could be a game-changer for the Raiders' offensive identity.

## Versatility and Grit: New Faces Bolstering the Raiders' Backfield Depth

Beyond the high-profile draft pick, the Raiders have been shrewd in adding complementary pieces to their running back unit.

### Roman Hemby and Connor Heyward: Adding New Dimensions to the Raiders' Run Game

Undrafted free agent **Roman Hemby** joins the fray after a highly productive collegiate career. Despite never being the sole "lead back," he amassed 2,347 rushing yards and 22 touchdowns over four seasons at Maryland, culminating in a career-high 1,120 yards and a national championship with Fernando Mendoza at Indiana. Hemby brings a proven ability to contribute consistently and could surprise in camp.

Adding another layer of versatility is fullback **Connor Heyward**, signed from the Pittsburgh Steelers. Heyward is the quintessential H-back, capable of rushing, blocking, and catching out of the backfield. With five career scrimmage touchdowns and valuable special teams contributions, he brings veteran savvy and a willingness to do the dirty work. Heyward himself noted the appeal of Coach Kubiak's scheme: "How he could use me – I watched the Seahawks Super Bowl... I knew he loved tight ends, he loved fullbacks, so this was just a perfect opportunity." This strategic signing highlights the Raiders' commitment to a multifaceted attack.

## Ashton Jeanty: The Reigning Rookie Star Focused on Elevating His Game

While the new faces bring excitement, the undisputed anchor of the Raiders' running back room is **Ashton Jeanty**. The 2025 first-round pick delivered a promising, albeit challenging, rookie campaign. Despite navigating multiple offensive line combinations and a midseason offensive coordinator change, Jeanty led all rookies in rushing yards (975) and scrimmage yards (1,321). His prowess as a pass-catcher was equally impressive, with 55 receptions and five receiving touchdowns, tying for second-most on the team.

Heading into Year 2, Jeanty isn't resting on his laurels. His focus is on refining the details: "Just focusing in more on the details," he stated, "what the defense is doing, how we're blocking things, coverage, all that type of stuff. So, it's definitely slowing down, and I think it's also just repetition as well. The more you rep it, the more the game slows down." This commitment to improvement, combined with his natural talent, suggests Jeanty is poised for an even bigger breakout season, cementing his role as the Raiders' lead back.

## Deep Dive into the Raiders' Running Back Depth and Special Teams Impact

Beyond the top trio, the Raiders maintain a competitive depth chart. **Chris Collier** returns after spending part of last season on the practice squad, signing a reserve/future contract in January. He'll be battling for reps and a roster spot.

### Dylan Laube: A Special Teams Ace Ready for More Opportunities

Perhaps the most overlooked but crucial piece in the depth puzzle is **Dylan Laube**, a 2024 sixth-round pick. While his carries were limited in 2025, his impact on special teams was undeniable. Laube averaged 25.9 yards per kick return, with his 855 kick return yards ranking 10th in the league. Special teams coordinator Joe DeCamillis praised Laube as "really, really sharp" and highlighted his understanding that versatility is key to making the squad. Laube's ability to flip field position could be invaluable, and he'll undoubtedly compete for more offensive snaps as well.

## Why This Loaded Backfield Matters for the Las Vegas Raiders in 2026

The Raiders are clearly prioritizing the run game, and for good reason. A robust ground attack is the heartbeat of a successful wide zone scheme, capable of:

*   **Controlling the clock:** Keeping opposing offenses off the field.
*   **Setting up play-action:** Opening up deep shots in the passing game.
*   **Protecting the quarterback:** Reducing pressure by establishing a run threat.
*   **Creating matchup nightmares:** The combination of Jeanty's power and receiving ability with Washington's explosive speed could force defenses to pick their poison.

Ashton Jeanty himself expressed excitement about Washington: "I think he's going to be a great player for us... just super excited to have him in the room." This camaraderie and complementary skill set could truly unlock the Raiders' offense. With a deeper, more versatile running back room, including a special teams dynamo in Laube and a blocking specialist in Heyward, the Las Vegas Raiders are building a foundation for a dominant 2026 season. The buzz around this backfield isn't just hype; it's a strategic move to redefine their attack and challenge the AFC. Get ready for some serious ground and pound.
