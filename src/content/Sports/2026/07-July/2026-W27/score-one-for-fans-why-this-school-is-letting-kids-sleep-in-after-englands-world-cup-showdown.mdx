Forget the usual Monday morning rush – for students at Bolton St Catherine’s Academy (BSCA), alarms can be snoozed a little longer. In a move that's quickly generating buzz, the Bolton school is giving its pupils a 90-minute reprieve, allowing them to arrive late on Monday, July 6, all for the love of the beautiful game.

This isn't just about a later start; it's about acknowledging a unique cultural moment, fostering family unity, and perhaps, even a little bit of national pride. As England faces Mexico in a critical World Cup knockout match with a 1 AM kick-off, BSCA is choosing to prioritize a shared national experience over rigid school schedules.

## Why Bolton St Catherine's Academy Is Letting Kids Sleep In After England's World Cup Match

The decision from Bolton St Catherine’s Academy, which encompasses both primary and secondary phases, is a direct response to England’s Round of 16 clash against Mexico. With the match kicking off at 1 AM and potentially stretching to 4 AM with extra time and penalties, headteachers Ryan Mallett (secondary) and Gareth Watson (primary) sent a clear message to parents: they want families to experience this together.

"While we maintain high standards for attendance and punctuality," their letter to parents explained, "we also recognise the importance of the opportunity for our pupils to watch the England match and celebrate with their families." This pragmatic approach means students across all year groups can arrive at 10 AM instead of the usual 8:35 AM. For parents who still require the regular start time, the school will operate as normal, providing flexibility for its 1,020 pupils.

It’s a balancing act: upholding educational standards while recognizing that some lessons extend beyond the classroom. This one-off policy change highlights the school's understanding of the profound impact major sporting events can have on community spirit and family life.

### Thomas Tuchel's Take: Why England Coach Backs Late School Starts for World Cup Viewers

This progressive stance from BSCA echoes sentiments recently shared by none other than England's coach, Thomas Tuchel. Following the Three Lions' narrow 2-1 victory over DR Congo, Tuchel was asked about the late-night viewing dilemma for young fans. His advice was unequivocal: "There’s so much school to go to, but the World Cup is every four years. Let them watch." He even suggested parents should "write an excuse for school and let them watch."

Tuchel's words underscore a growing recognition that global events like the World Cup are not just sporting contests, but significant cultural phenomena. They create indelible memories, forge national identity, and offer rare opportunities for collective celebration. The Bolton school's decision could be seen as a direct endorsement of this philosophy, allowing students to be part of history in the making.

### Beyond the Classroom: The Cultural Impact of World Cup Viewing on Families

The ripple effect of a major sporting event like the World Cup extends far beyond the stadium. For many families, watching England play becomes a cherished ritual – a chance to bond, to share excitement, and to experience the highs and lows of national pride together. These moments, particularly when they are few and far between (the World Cup indeed happens only every four years), hold a unique value.

By making this accommodation, Bolton St Catherine’s Academy isn't just granting a later start; it's validating these shared experiences. It's acknowledging that life outside of school contributes significantly to a child's development and well-being. This move sparks a broader conversation about flexibility in education and the importance of allowing space for significant cultural and emotional events.

In an age where digital distractions often pull families apart, the communal experience of cheering on the national team, even in the dead of night, offers a powerful antidote. For the kids of Bolton, Monday morning might come with a touch more sleep and a whole lot more World Cup memories.
