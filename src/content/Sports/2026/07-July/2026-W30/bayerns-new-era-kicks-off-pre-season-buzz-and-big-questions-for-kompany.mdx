## FC Bayern Kicks Off Pre-Season: What to Expect from Kompany's Era?

The silence is over. Just 58 days after clinching the DFB-Pokal, and a mere blink after the World Cup final, FC Bayern Munich has officially begun its pre-season. For fans of Germany's reigning double-winners, this isn't just another calendar entry; it's the first tangible step into the Vincent Kompany era, a journey fraught with immediate challenges, high expectations, and a squad that will assemble in waves. The early buzz is less about spectacular transfers (for now) and more about the nitty-gritty: medical tests, injury rehabilitation, and the strategic integration of a diverse roster. Why does this matter? Because how Bayern handles these initial weeks will lay the groundwork for their entire season.

### Who Reported First for Bayern's Rigorous Medical Tests?

Monday morning, 7:43 AM: the unofficial start of a new Bayern chapter. While most of the world's football elite were still basking in post-World Cup glory (or commiserating), seasoned goalkeeper Sven Ulreich (37) was the first to pull into the underground garage of the Barmherzige Brüder hospital in Munich. Ulreich, who recently extended his contract alongside Manuel Neuer, set a professional tone, arriving even before the kit managers.

Joining him for the initial battery of tests, which included lung function analyses and stress EKGs, was Arijon Ibrahimovic (20). The attacking all-rounder returns from his loan spell at Heidenheim and is reportedly firmly in Kompany's plans for the upcoming campaign. Alongside these two, only young talents Tom Bischof (21) and Lennart Karl (18) were present. The glaring absence of other big names? Most of Bayern's World Cup participants are still enjoying their well-deserved extended holidays.

### Bayern's Injury Updates: When Will Gnabry and Musiala Return?

A significant portion of the early pre-season focus falls on Bayern's wounded warriors. Lennart Karl, still recovering from a severe muscle tear in his thigh sustained six weeks prior during World Cup preparations, continues his rehabilitation. More notably, star winger Serge Gnabry (31), sidelined for 13 weeks with an adductor tear, made his appearance for the medical checks sporting a fresh dreadlock hairstyle. He was joined by young talent David Santos Daiber (19), also in rehab for a muscle tendon tear.

Perhaps the most anticipated return is that of Jamal Musiala (23), who underwent surgery in July 2025 to remove a metal plate following a tibia and fibula fracture with ankle injury. Musiala, along with Kane, Olise, Upamecano, Davies, and new signing Ismael Saibari, will only rejoin the squad *after* the Asia tour, highlighting the staggered approach to reintegrating key players. Gnabry and Karl will also remain in Munich for their intensive rehabilitation programs, skipping the early travel. This piecemeal return poses a real challenge for Kompany in building early team cohesion.

## Bayern's Loan Returnees: Will Palhinha, Boey, and Zaragoza Stay or Go?

The medical queue also featured three players whose futures at the club are far from certain: João Palhinha (31), Sacha Boey (25), and Bryan Zaragoza (24). While the original text indicated their previous loan spells, it's widely known that Palhinha was a long-standing transfer target for Bayern from Fulham, not "zuletzt Tottenham." Regardless of their past affiliations, these players represent potential transfer income or crucial squad depth if Kompany finds a role for them. Their presence in pre-season signals a decision point for the club – integrate, loan out again, or sell to fund other ventures. The transfer market is heating up, and Bayern's strategy for these individuals could dictate further moves.

### The Challenge of Integrating a Staggered FC Bayern Squad

With key players returning at different intervals, Kompany faces a complex logistical and tactical puzzle. The DFB stars – Neuer, Urbig, Kimmich, Tah, Pavlovic, and new signing Nathaniel Brown (reportedly from Frankfurt for €50m, a significant investment) – along with new defensive additions Hiroki Ito (28, Japan) and Minjae Kim (29, South Korea), will join the team at the Tegernsee training camp. Further international players like Konrad Laimer (29, Austria), Josip Stanisic (26, Croatia), and Luis Díaz (29, Colombia) will integrate before the marketing-focused Asia tour.

This phased approach means Kompany will have limited time with his full squad before competitive matches begin. Building chemistry, implementing new tactics, and fostering a unified team spirit under these conditions will be a critical early test for the new coach.

## FC Bayern's Pre-Season Schedule: Key Dates, Training Camp, and Supercup Showdown

Despite the staggered returns, Bayern's pre-season calendar is packed:

*   **July 25th:** First test match against third-division side SV Wehen Wiesbaden (3:30 PM).
*   **July 27th-30th:** Training camp at Tegernsee, featuring three public training sessions (July 27, 28, 29 at 3:30 PM at Stadion am Birkenmoos) and a friendly match against Kreisklasse club FC Rottach-Egern (July 30th).
*   **August 1st-8th:** Asia tour to South Korea and Hong Kong, a crucial marketing expedition where the full squad still won't be present.

### All Eyes on the Franz-Beckenbauer-Supercup Against Borussia Dortmund

The real fireworks begin in August. The final pre-season tune-up is set for **August 15th** at the Allianz Arena, the Telekom-Cup against rivals RB Leipzig (3:30 PM). However, the first official taste of competitive action, and a significant early psychological battle, comes on **August 22nd** in Dortmund. Bayern will face arch-rivals Borussia Dortmund in the Franz-Beckenbauer-Supercup. This match isn't just about a trophy; it's a statement, an early marker for the season.

The Bundesliga campaign kicks off on **August 28th** (8:30 PM) with a home game against VfB Stuttgart. From medicals to Supercup, the road ahead for FC Bayern is challenging, exciting, and demands immediate focus. The Kompany era has officially begun, and the football world is watching.
