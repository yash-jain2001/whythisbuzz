## Royal Birkdale: A Monument to Golfing Grandeur and Record-Breaking Feats

Royal Birkdale isn't just a golf course; it's a crucible where legends are forged and history is etched into the very dunes. Nestled north of Liverpool, this storied links venue, founded in 1889, has hosted The Open a staggering 11 times and the Women's Open seven. Known for its towering dunes, deep fairway bunkers, and classic links challenges, Birkdale demands nothing short of perfection.

But even on such a formidable stage, moments of sheer brilliance break through – rounds so exceptional they become the stuff of legend. We're talking about the course records, those seemingly impossible scores that defy the elements and the pressure of championship golf. Let's dive into the two jaw-dropping performances that cemented their place in Royal Birkdale's, and indeed, golf's history books.

### The Legendary Links of Royal Birkdale: History and Challenge

Since its first Open Championship in 1954, won by Australian icon Peter Thompson, Royal Birkdale has consistently delivered drama. The course is renowned for its architectural integrity, punishing errant shots and rewarding strategic play. It’s a true test of nerve and skill, making any low score here not just good, but *historic*. The current men's and women's course records stand as monuments to human perseverance and golfing prowess against this magnificent backdrop.

## Branden Grace's Historic 62: The Unforgettable Men's Major Record

The year was 2017. The Open Championship was in full swing at Royal Birkdale, and a relatively unheralded South African, **Branden Grace**, was about to redefine what was possible in men's Major golf.

### How Branden Grace Carded Golf's First-Ever 62 at The Open

Going into his third round, Grace was a distant figure on the leaderboard, sitting at four-over-par and a daunting ten shots behind then-leader Jordan Spieth. What happened next wasn't just a comeback; it was an explosion of birdies that rewrote history.

Grace later explained the psychological edge: "For me it's a big thing if I get off to making a good putt on the first hole, I feel good about the putting for the rest of the round." And boy, did he get off to a good start! A 15-foot birdie putt on the first hole sparked a fire that would burn all day.

### Anatomy of a Perfect Round: Grace's Dominant Performance

After a couple of routine pars, Grace drained an astonishing 40-foot birdie putt on the fourth, putting him two-under through four holes. The momentum was palpable. He drove the fifth green for another birdie, then added two more on the eighth and ninth, turning in a blistering five-under-par 29.

The back nine saw no let-up. Four consecutive pars kept the round ticking along before another lengthy putt dropped on the par-three 14th, bringing him to six-under with just four holes remaining. While a birdie eluded him on the par-five 15th, Grace quickly rectified it with another perfectly sunk putt on the 16th. Finishing with a birdie on the 17th and a par on the 18th, Grace signed for an incredible **bogey-free 62**, eight-under-par.

This wasn't just a new course record, beating the previous best by one shot; it was a watershed moment: **the first time a golfer had ever carded a 62 in the history of men's Major championships.** Grace combined composed course management with a sublime putting display, showing precisely *why* he deserved a place in golf's pantheon.

## Morgan Pressel's Resilient 65: A Women's Open Masterclass

While Branden Grace's 62 stunned the men's game, the women's course record at Royal Birkdale tells a story of an equally remarkable comeback and resilience. In 2010, during the Women's Open, America's **Morgan Pressel** delivered a round that proved sheer willpower can overcome even the most frustrating starts.

### Overcoming Adversity: Pressel's Epic Comeback at Royal Birkdale

Pressel's third round began inauspiciously. Starting at four-over-par, back-to-back bogeys on her opening two holes pushed her to six-over. For many, this would be a round to forget, a slide down the leaderboard. But for Pressel, it was merely the prelude to an extraordinary turnaround.

From that moment, the American's game ignited. She embarked on a furious charge, racking up eight birdies and an eagle. Her quote perfectly encapsulates the mindset: "I made a lot of putts today, if you can hit greens out there you can score pretty well." And score she did! Pressel went on to shoot a stunning **seven-under-par 65**, etching her name into Birkdale's history as the women's course record holder. It was a testament to her belief and clutch putting under pressure.
