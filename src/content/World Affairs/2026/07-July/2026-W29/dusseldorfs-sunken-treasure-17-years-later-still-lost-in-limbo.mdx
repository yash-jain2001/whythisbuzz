## Düsseldorf's Priceless Shipwreck: A 17-Year Wait for the Spotlight

Imagine unearthing a piece of history so rare, so perfectly preserved, it rewrites the books. Now imagine that priceless artifact gathering dust in a city archive for nearly two decades. Welcome to the perplexing saga of the Kaiserswerth "Nachen" – an ancient flat-bottomed boat that has become a symbol of both archaeological triumph and bureaucratic stagnation in Düsseldorf.

Discovered and salvaged back in 2009, this unique shipwreck from the early modern period has been lauded by experts as an unparalleled find. Yet, 17 years on, its future remains adrift, leaving many to wonder why Düsseldorf's cultural jewel is still out of sight.

### Unearthing the Past: Why the Kaiserswerth Nachen Is So Significant

"You really can't overestimate the importance of this find," raves Hans-Peter Schletter, the archaeologist who led the original excavation in Kaiserswerth. Now based at the Burg Linn Museum in Krefeld, Schletter's enthusiasm for the Nachen is palpable. "Its completeness is simply unmatched."

Schletter has dedicated scholarly articles to the find, firmly believing the Nachen originated from the Upper Rhine, likely Strasbourg. Its presence in Kaiserswerth, he suggests, dates back to the French siege of the city, serving either as a troop transporter or a vital supply vessel. What makes it truly extraordinary is that it’s not just the *only* surviving example of its type, but it's also astonishingly well-preserved, offering a direct window into 17th-century riverine transport and warfare.

### From Riverbed to Archive: The Journey of the Ancient Vessel

The arduous task of bringing this relic to light was a triumph of archaeological engineering. The shipwreck was painstakingly excavated, meticulously documented, and then transported to Schleswig-Holstein for a complex and lengthy conservation process. By 2021, the conserved individual components made their way back to the Rhine, settling not into a grand exhibition hall, but into a quiet city archive. And there it has largely stayed.

### Düsseldorf's Cultural Conundrum: Why Is the Shipwreck Still Hidden?

For months, local media, including WDR, have pressed the City of Düsseldorf for answers regarding the fate of this historically invaluable discovery. Repeated requests for interviews with the head of the city's Maritime Museum have been met with a wall of silence from the municipal communications office. Written responses, when they do arrive, paint a picture of ongoing deliberation rather than concrete plans.

"A final exhibition location for the Kaiserswerth Nachen has not yet been determined," states an unnamed communication office employee. "The shipwreck is intended to be presented in a museum context; the conceptual development and the concrete public presentation are currently under review." While this sounds like forward motion, it’s also the kind of statement that can keep a treasure hidden for years.

The good news? Funding appears to be in place for personnel costs related to reconstruction. A substantial €340,000 in third-party funding has been approved by the Regional Cultural Promotion of the Landschaftsverband Rheinland (LVR). But money for staff doesn't automatically translate to an exhibition.

### The Decades-Long Challenge: Displaying Ancient Shipwrecks Requires Constant Climate Control

Schletter highlights a crucial, often underestimated challenge: the technical requirements for displaying such an artifact. "You need a constant room climate. And you have to guarantee that for decades." He speaks from experience, standing next to a displayed shipwreck in the Burg Linn Museum – a vessel found in 1973 that took a staggering 30 years to be publicly exhibited. In Krefeld's case, it was civic engagement and two dedicated associations, rather than city funding alone, that ultimately made the display possible.

### Breathing New Life into a Landmark: The Pump House Proposal for Kaiserswerth

Could a similar spark ignite for the Kaiserswerth Nachen? Jan Hinnerk Meyer, an architect and chairman of the Förderverein Kaiserpfalz (Kaiserpfalz Support Association), certainly hopes so. He envisions a synergistic solution: showcasing the Nachen within a new structure that integrates with the historic, yet currently dilapidated, old pump house on the Rhine dike.

This architectural marvel would serve as an entrance to a purpose-built exhibition space, just meters from where the wreck was originally found. "It couldn't be more brilliant," Meyer exclaims, looking at the listed pump house that today stands unused and in a regrettable state. He believes this location would attract not only dedicated history buffs but also casual visitors strolling or cycling along the dike.

### Will Citizens & Businesses Help Exhibit Düsseldorf's Historic Ship?

Meyer has been actively lobbying local politicians for his innovative idea, but his biggest hope lies in the power of community. He's appealing for the engagement of citizens and businesses to secure the long-term exhibition of the Nachen at this poignant location. "I am confident that we can get people not only to advocate for this but also to participate economically," he asserts.

The Kaiserswerth Nachen isn't just an old boat; it's a profound link to Düsseldorf's past, a testament to ancient craftsmanship, and a potential catalyst for local tourism and cultural pride. The question now is whether the city, its citizens, and its businesses can come together to finally bring this sunken treasure out of the shadows and into the light it so richly deserves.
