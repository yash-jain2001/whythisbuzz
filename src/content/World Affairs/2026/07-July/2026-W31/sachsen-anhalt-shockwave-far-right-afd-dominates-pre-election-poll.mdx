## Sachsen-Anhalt Election Nears: AfD's Dominance Confirmed by New Poll

Just six weeks before voters head to the polls in Sachsen-Anhalt, a new survey has sent a seismic jolt through Germany's political landscape. The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD), controversially classified as "definitely right-wing extremist" by the state's domestic intelligence agency, is holding an overwhelming lead at a staggering 41%.

This isn't just a bump; it's a political earthquake. Commissioned by a consortium of major broadcasters and newspapers including MDR, WDR, Mitteldeutsche Zeitung, and Volksstimme, the Infratest dimap poll paints a stark picture of voter sentiment, pushing the traditional parties into an unprecedented corner.

### AfD's Electoral Surge: What the Latest Sachsen-Anhalt Numbers Reveal

The numbers are clear: the AfD's 41% isn't just a high-water mark, it's a *doubling* of their support since the 2021 state election where they secured 20.8%. This sustained momentum shows the party isn't just capitalizing on momentary frustrations but cementing a significant base of power in the eastern German state. This isn't just about protest votes; it's about a deep shift in voter allegiances that traditional parties are struggling to comprehend, let alone counter.

### CDU's Historic Decline: Analyzing the Erosion of Traditional Support

On the other other side of the ledger, the center-right CDU, once the dominant force in Sachsen-Anhalt, continues its freefall. Dropping another two points since May, they now stand at a mere 24%. To put this into perspective, the CDU commanded 37.1% just five years ago – meaning they've lost well over a third of their voters. This dramatic decline speaks volumes about a growing disillusionment with mainstream politics and a potential crisis of identity for the party.

Other parties also saw shifts:
*   **The Left (Die Linke)**: Up one point to 13%.
*   **SPD (Social Democrats)**: Holding steady at 7%.
*   **BSW (Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht)**: Stable at 4%, just below the electoral threshold.
*   **Greens (Bündnis 90/Die Grünen)**: Gaining a point to reach 5%, scraping past the threshold.
*   **FDP (Free Democrats)**: Remains below the 5% hurdle, indicating continued struggles for liberals in the region.

## Navigating the Political Minefield: Sachsen-Anhalt's Coalition Predicament

With the AfD's commanding lead, the question of who will actually govern Sachsen-Anhalt becomes a political Gordian knot. The raw numbers suggest a direct AfD-led government could be possible, but the complexities run much deeper. When asked which party should lead the government, the AfD (44%) and CDU (43%) are neck and neck, revealing a deeply divided electorate even among those who prefer other parties, where CDU leadership is largely favored.

### CDU-AfD Alliance: The "Brandmauer" Holds (Mostly) Among Voters

One of the most pressing questions is the "Brandmauer" – the metaphorical firewall preventing cooperation with the AfD. The poll reveals a nuanced picture:
*   **Overall Rejection**: A significant 60% of all eligible voters reject a CDU-AfD coalition, with only 33% in favor. This is a clear signal from the broader electorate against such an alliance.
*   **CDU Base's Stance**: Crucially, *82% of CDU supporters* explicitly reject a coalition with the AfD. This demonstrates a strong commitment within the party's base to uphold the firewall, despite internal debates and national pressures. Only 16% of CDU voters would support it.
*   **AfD Base's Stance**: Interestingly, even among AfD supporters, the desire for a CDU-AfD coalition isn't universal. While 55% favor it, a substantial 39% reject the idea themselves. This internal division highlights the complexities of forming any government in this fractured landscape.

### Other Potential Partnerships: Can the Political Gridlock Be Broken?

A coalition between the CDU and The Left also lacks popular support, with 61% against it and only 31% in favor. However, the CDU base is more divided on this front, with 41% of their supporters open to a Left alliance, compared to 52% who are against it. This suggests that while a left-leaning partnership might be less popular overall, it faces less entrenched ideological opposition within the CDU's ranks than an AfD alliance.

The current numbers hint at a potential multi-party alliance – perhaps a "Kenya" coalition (CDU, SPD, Greens) or "Germany" coalition (CDU, SPD, FDP) – as the only viable path to a stable government *without* the AfD. But the path to agreement will be fraught with challenges.

## Deepening Dissatisfaction: Why Sachsen-Anhalt Voters Are Unhappy with Current Government

Beyond the party numbers, the poll underscores a profound dissatisfaction with the current state government. Only 29% of respondents expressed satisfaction with its performance, a four-point drop since May. Conversely, a whopping 66% are less or not at all satisfied, with the deeply unhappy contingent rising to 28%. This widespread discontent is a major driver behind the AfD's surge, with a staggering 90% of AfD supporters expressing unhappiness, compared to a more moderate 25% among CDU voters.

### Rising Stars: How AfD's Siegmund and CDU's Schulze Are Gaining Traction

Despite the overall governmental dissatisfaction, individual leaders are seeing their stock rise. Ulrich Siegmund, the AfD's lead candidate, has seen a remarkable 15-point jump in satisfaction since September 2025, now standing at 35%. Not to be outdone, CDU lead candidate Sven Schulze also improved by nine points to 29%. While the incumbent Minister-President remains the most recognized (65%), Siegmund is hot on his heels at 63%, indicating the AfD's increasing visibility and public acceptance of its key figures.

## Beyond the Numbers: The Broader Impact of Sachsen-Anhalt's Political Landscape

The situation in Sachsen-Anhalt is more than just a regional issue; it's a bellwether for Germany's political future. The deep divisions, the rise of the far-right, and the struggles of established parties highlight challenges felt across the nation. Lingering concerns over issues like doctor shortages and ongoing debates within Berlin's political circles about national leadership are clearly impacting the state election dynamics. As the September 6th election approaches, the world will be watching to see how Sachsen-Anhalt navigates this complex and potentially transformative moment in its political history.

### Understanding the Data: Infratest dimap's Sachsen-Anhalt Trend Methodology

The Infratest dimap poll was conducted between July 23rd and 28th, 2026, surveying 1,149 eligible voters through a combination of telephone and online interviews. The margin of error is +/- 2 points for parties at 10% and +/- 3 points for parties at 50%. This robust methodology provides a reliable snapshot of the current political mood in Sachsen-Anhalt.
